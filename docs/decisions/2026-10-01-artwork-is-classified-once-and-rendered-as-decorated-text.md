# Artwork is classified once and rendered as decorated text

*Decided September 2026 (issue #31; design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-artwork-renderers-design.md`).*
Which renderer a verbatim block goes to is decided in one place, `ArtworkClassifier` in RFCKit, and never by a renderer guessing at its own kind.
The first of these that applies wins: a reviewed hint that says the block is nothing; the block's own `type`, lowercased and without media-type parameters (`ArtworkType`); a hint's type where the block's is generic, `ascii-art` above all; an exact recognizer, of which `PacketDiagram` is the first; otherwise nothing, and the block is set as it always was.
The classification sits beside the model and is never written into it, because the reader prints a source block's `type` as its label and the converter writes it back out.
Hints are keyed by an RFC and a block's `pn`, which a published RFC never changes, and ship compiled into the app (`ArtworkHints.bundled`, empty so far); converted documents take none, since their blocks move whenever the legacy parser improves, and get their types from the converter instead.

`ArtworkRenderers` in RFCReaderKit maps each type to its presentations in order of preference, and the first that accepts a block renders it.
A packet diagram is rendered as *decorated text*: the block's own characters, set exactly as before, with the characters that draw its grid in a clear color, its ruler in the secondary color, and real lines drawn in their place.
The lines are strokes on the block's monospace grid in half cells (`Stroke`), independent of font and scale, placed in each line's fragment by `StrokeGeometry` and drawn by `RFCTextLayoutFragment`.
Because nothing in the storage changes but attributes, find still matches a field name inside a diagram, a selection copies the ASCII, VoiceOver reads text (and on macOS says a rendered packet diagram as its fields), and a long diagram breaks across printed pages line by line.
A drawing that cannot be text, such as a railroad diagram, would give all of that up, which is why the first renderer is not one.
Every rendered block can be shown as its text instead ("Show as Text", and back with "Show as Figure"), from its menu: the context menu on macOS, and on iOS a long press, which lifts the figure as it is drawn over a menu of that item, Copy and Share, the way Photos and Safari handle an image.
On iOS the long press reaches the text view's delegate because the builder makes the block's body one UIKit text item (`rfcFigureItem`, the text item tag), so it goes through the same `menuConfigurationFor` as a reference's preview and needs no gesture of its own.
The item UIKit hands over is no longer than the storage run under the finger, and a drawn diagram's grid and ruler colors cut its body into runs a line or less long, so the menu lifts the item's whole extent from the storage (`FigureMenu.itemRange`), not the item's range: drawn from that, the lifted figure was the line pressed.
A tap on a figure is still a tap on text, which the bars' recognizer takes; the item's primary action is one that does nothing, because for an item with none UIKit opens the menu on a tap.
A whole-document preference, "Draw diagrams" in Settings on the Mac and in the Aa menu on iOS (`ReaderPreferences.drawDiagramsKey`), decides how every such block is shown, and a choice from a block's menu overrides it for that block (`PresentationChoices`).
A version with a control over the content came before it and was dropped: a round glass Figure | Source button in the card's corner, shown on hover on macOS and always on iOS, and before that a drawn pill and a segmented control.
Apple puts no controls over content being read; it puts them in the content's own menu and in settings, which is where this one went.
A verbatim block's card ends a padding past its widest line (`.rfcContentWidth`) rather than at the column's edge, and a rendered diagram is indented so that its card sits in the middle of the column; source code and plain artwork keep their indent.
A choice from a menu is held per document for the session in `LibraryModel`, keyed by the block's anchor, which names the same block in every build, or by its place among the document's verbatim blocks where it has none (`PresentationKey`), and reaches the builder through the build inputs, so print and export show each block as the reader does, while a force-click preview shows every block rendered, whatever the preference.
