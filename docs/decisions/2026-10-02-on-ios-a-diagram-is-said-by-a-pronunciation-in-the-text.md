# On iOS, a diagram is said by a pronunciation in the text

*Decided October 2026 (issues #12, #308), to be confirmed by ear on a device.*
On macOS VoiceOver says a diagram as one label, "Diagram", because `ReaderTextView` overrides the per-range accessibility accessors (#297).
`UITextView` has no such accessor to override, so that fix cannot be copied, and VoiceOver on iOS read a diagram one box-drawing character at a time.
The only lever that keeps the text view one accessibility element, with its line, word and character reading, selection and in-place links, is a speech attribute in the text itself: the builder sets `accessibilitySpeechIPANotation` on a diagram's lines, the IPA of "diagram" on the first line that is not blank and an empty pronunciation on the later ones (`AccessibleReading.diagramSpeech`).

A diagram as its own accessibility element was weighed and rejected: the text view would have to become a container, losing word and character granularity, selection and in-place links in all the prose around it. Only "diagram" is said, not a packet diagram's field list as on macOS, because its fields cannot be turned into IPA.

Whether VoiceOver honors a speech attribute in a text view's storage is not documented, and an empty pronunciation least of all, so this waits for a listening pass on a device.
If line-by-line reading still reads the characters, a single space or another silent pronunciation is the next try.
If reading through does not say "diagram" either, VoiceOver does not honor the attribute in a text view, and the limitation is documented instead.

## How a diagram is said on both platforms

*Moved here from ARCHITECTURE.md's known gaps on 17 October 2026 (#756).*

On macOS, VoiceOver says a diagram as one label, "Diagram", in place of its box drawing. Not `Preformatted.name`, which is RFCXML's file name to extract the artwork to, not a title. A diagram is artwork that is a drawing (`AccessibleReading.isDiagram`): artwork typed as one (`ascii-art`, which the legacy parser sets, #361), or untyped artwork whose shape says so (`DrawingShape`): at least 0.3 of its characters other than white space draw lines, boxes or arrows, and no more than half of its non-blank lines are words — a letter, and more than half letters and digits — because legacy documents set every block that is not prose as artwork, and a grammar, a message example or a table reads perfectly well as words. A bordered table therefore needs more rows than borders to be read; one with a header and two data rows is still said as a diagram, because a bit layout alternates field rows and borders just as closely. `ReaderTextView` overrides the per-range accessibility accessors, which hand their `super` to `AccessibleReading.reading` (RFCReaderKit), while `accessibilityValue` stays the real text, because every range VoiceOver asks for is measured in its length. The label is said where a range reaches the diagram's first character, so read a line at a time the first line announces it and the rest are silent. It is not the figure's caption, which in a document from XML is set as text right after the diagram and would be read twice; a legacy document's "Figure 3: …" line is its figure's title as well (#361). Reading through, VoiceOver no longer reaches the ASCII; only what bypasses the per-range accessors still carries it, `accessibilityValue` and `accessibilitySelectedText`. The Diagrams rotor jumps to a diagram and lists it by its figure's caption (`.rfcCaption`, `AccessibleReading.rotorLabel`), which the rotor never reads through, or as "Diagram" without one; Copy Figure puts the ASCII on the pasteboard. On iOS, `UITextView` has no per-range accessor to override, so the builder says it in the text instead (#308): a diagram's first line that is not blank carries the IPA of "diagram" as `accessibilitySpeechIPANotation`, and its later lines an empty pronunciation (`AccessibleReading.diagramSpeech`, in reader builds only), line breaks and blank lines nothing. Only "diagram", since a packet diagram's fields cannot be made IPA. Whether VoiceOver honors a speech attribute in a text view's storage, reading through and line by line, is not documented, and is to be confirmed by ear on a device, as is what character and word navigation inside a diagram's line says now: the pronunciation covers the whole line, so it may be said for every character of it. Why it is said in the text and not by an accessibility element of its own is [the iOS diagram speech decision](decisions/2026-10-02-on-ios-a-diagram-is-said-by-a-pronunciation-in-the-text.md).
