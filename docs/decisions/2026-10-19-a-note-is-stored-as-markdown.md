# A note is stored as Markdown

*19 October 2026, #523, slice 2 of the highlights and notes design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-highlights-and-notes-design.md`, #528).*

## The body is restricted CommonMark

A reading note's `body` is a Markdown string: paragraphs, bulleted and numbered lists, nested, and inside them emphasis, strong, strikethrough, inline code and links. What the editor edits is a projection of it, made when it opens and turned back when it saves. The editor's own format was not stored, for the design's two reasons: a TextKit list lives in `NSParagraphStyle`, not in an `AttributedString` scope, so the stored format would have depended on which editor won; and a custom scope's `Codable` drops attributes it does not know without an error, so a renamed key would lose formatting silently. Markdown depends on neither editor, diffs sensibly under sync, makes the Markdown export (slice 5a) close to an identity, and is what the PDF export (slice 5b) writes into an annotation.

`NoteBody` is the scope as a value: blocks of paragraphs and lists, an item one paragraph and its nested lists, spans of text in a style with a link. It is kept canonical, so that two bodies that serialize alike are equal, and parse ∘ serialize is the identity on every body (`NoteMarkdownTests`, over every construct of the scope and the characters Markdown would read as something else). Parsing is Foundation's CommonMark parser, `AttributedString(markdown:)` with its full syntax, reduced to the scope: a heading, quote, code block or table becomes paragraphs of its text. Serializing is ours, in the one spelling the parser reads back: `*`, `**` and `~~` opened and closed as a stack, white space kept outside them, a code span fenced past its longest run of backticks, every character with a meaning escaped, and a URL's `://` and a `www.` escaped, since the parser's autolink extension would otherwise make text a link. What the scope cannot keep is normalized away, not lost on a round trip: an empty paragraph or item, white space at a paragraph's edges, formatting inside a link's text (the parser keeps none there, so the link takes its run's common style), and two lists of one kind side by side, which Markdown cannot tell from one. A mail address is the one thing no escape keeps from becoming a link; it reads back as a `mailto:` link.

## The editor is TextKit 2, not `TextEditor`

The design made the editor a probe. SwiftUI's `TextEditor` over `AttributedString` drew a nested list parsed from Markdown as its items run together, with no `NSTextList` on any paragraph, and Return in it inserted a bare line break: it has no lists. A TextKit 2 `NSTextView` with `NSTextList` paragraphs, the markers left out of the text as TextKit 2 draws them, did what Notes does: Return continues the list, Tab nests the item, Shift-Tab takes it out, Return on an empty item ends the list. So the editor is `NSTextView` on the Mac and `UITextView` on iOS, behind one SwiftUI view (`NoteEditor`), and `NoteText` is the projection both ways, under test: a paragraph a line, an item a paragraph whose style holds its lists, a line break U+2028. Reading text back is also how a paste is reduced to the scope: bold, italic, a monospaced font, a strikethrough, a link and a list survive it; fonts, sizes, colors and headings do not. The formatting buttons, ⌘B, ⌘I and ⌘K are `NoteText`'s too, so iPhone, without Tab, can still nest an item.

## A note's owner, and its orphans

A note names a highlight (`highlightIdentifier`) or has a target of its own: a section, or a range with its quote, as a highlight does; a note on a figure, table or paragraph is a range covering exactly that block (`NoteBlock`). A note on a highlight also keeps the highlight's range and quote as they were when it was made, in the range fields: the spec's orphan rule, that a note whose highlight is gone goes to the surviving highlight that covers its quote, needs a quote to go by, and a note made under sync can arrive without its highlight. `NotePlacement` decides it per build, and the app writes the new owner back, as it does a relocation, without touching `modifiedAt`. With no highlight covering it, the note is kept and listed apart as detached. A merge re-points the absorbed highlights' notes to the survivor, and deleting a highlight deletes its notes, after asking; both are undone with the highlights.

A note is made the first time it has text, and deleted when its text is gone, so a note left empty is never kept. It is saved a second after typing pauses and when the editor closes.

## Markers stand by real layout only

A note's marker sits in the trailing gutter, level with the first line of what it is on (`FragmentGeometry.noteMarkers`). Its line comes from the layout fragment holding the target's first character, and only from one TextKit has laid out: a fragment's frame outside the laid-out range is an estimate, so a marker there waits until the layout engine reaches it (`ReaderLayoutEngine.didLayOut`) or a scroll brings it in. Notes of one line share a marker, which lists them; one that would overlap the marker above moves beneath it. The markers are buttons over the text view, as the header is, never part of the storage, and nothing they do reports back into the text's layout.

`SchemaV7` adds the `Note` rows. #906 (following a working group) adds a `SchemaV6` of its own: whichever of it and this stack merges second renumbers its versions.
