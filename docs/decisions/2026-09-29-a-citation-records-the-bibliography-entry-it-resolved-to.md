# A citation records the bibliography entry it resolved to

*Decided September 2026 (issue #184).* Whether a citation is normative or informative is the kind of the list holding its entry, and the kind comes from the list's title (`ReferenceList.Kind`, derived, never stored; numbering, a trailing colon and a trailing parenthetical such as an ordering note are set aside, the rest of the title must match whole). For a citation by anchor the entry is the anchor. For a `.document` citation the renderer used to find the entry by the document it names, and that fails exactly where RFCXML groups: a `<referencegroup>` is one entry in its list, under the group's anchor and series (BCP 26), so a citation of its member RFC 8126 named a document no entry's series names. Measured over the XML corpus, that left 106 citations in 22 documents with no kind. Guessing the group from its members at render time would have been a second resolution beside the parser's, so the parser, which already resolves the citation (see "Cross references are resolved at parse time" in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md)), records the entry it resolved to on the target, `entry`: the reference's own anchor, or a member's group's. The legacy parser records the entry a `[label]` citation resolved to in the same way; neither records one for a bare mention the bibliography did not match, which falls back to finding the entry by document. With `entry`, all 106 citations get their group's kind and no citation in the corpus loses one; with the parenthetical set aside, RFC 3543's two lists and their 53 citations get one too.

`entry` is part of the target's equality, so two citations of one document through different entries are different targets. `RFCXMLSerializer` writes a citation against its `entry` when that entry names the cited document; a `<referencegroup>`'s member records its group, which names another, and written against the group it read back as the group's document. Otherwise, and for a bare mention, which records no entry, it writes the entry anchored under the document itself (`RFC7159`), or else the first entry naming it. It used to write the first entry always, and in 569 legacy documents that was not the one the prose cited (887 citations, such as an erratum listed before the RFC it corrects). Writing `entry` waited on a second fix (#424): the legacy parser read an entry's RFC from anywhere in it, its title included, so RFC 8174's entry, whose title is about the keywords of RFC 2119, named RFC 2119, and `[RFC8174]` would have read as written while linking to RFC 2119. An entry's RFC, BCP and STD are now read from the entry with its quoted title left out: 633 entries in 593 documents change, 392 of them RFC 8174's, and 44 now name none. 41 of those are right, a draft, a mailing-list message or an erratum whose title mentions an RFC; 3 are RFCs whose authors put the series inside the quotes (RFC 1938's `[2]`, RFC 1650's `[11]` and `[19]`), which no rule tells apart from a title about an RFC, so they wait for #197.

A group's members resolve through their group. A bare mention in prose ("Section 4.1 of RFC 8126") is linked without the bibliography, so it records no `entry`, and the fallback by document found nothing for a group's member: the group's entry carries only the group's own series. The XML parser therefore keeps the members' documents on the group's `Reference` (`members`), and `ReferenceKinds` indexes them beside the entry's own document. Measured over the XML corpus: 111 bare mentions of a group's member in 25 documents had no kind, and all 111 now have their group's; no mention's kind changed otherwise.
