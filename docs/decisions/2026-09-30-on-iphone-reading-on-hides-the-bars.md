# On iPhone, reading on hides the bars

*Decided September 2026.* In a single column the top and bottom bars cover a fifth of the screen. Reading on puts both away, as Books and Safari do; a tap on the text, a scroll back, either end of the document and a jump to a place bring them back. When to do which is `ReaderChrome` in RFCReaderKit, a value fed by the text view's scrolls and taps; the view only applies it. UIKit's `hidesBarsOnSwipe` and `hidesBarsOnTap` were not used: the navigation controller is SwiftUI's, SwiftUI's bottom bar is not its toolbar, and a tap on the text is usually a tap on a link. A tap on the header's blank space counts as one on the text: the hosted header's root carries a tap gesture, which its author chips and banner links, SwiftUI gestures beneath it, take precedence over, so a tap on one of them does only what it does. Only a scroll a finger makes hides or shows by its direction. A jump or a restored place moves where the next run is measured from and nothing else. Not beside other columns, and not under VoiceOver.

The top bar goes with `.toolbarVisibility(.hidden)`, on an ease-in-out curve: a spring made it fly off the screen. The bottom bar goes by losing its items (`DocumentToolbar.showsBottomBar`), which dissolve in place. Swiping back does not work while the top bar is hidden, which is UIKit's own behavior for a hidden navigation bar, and is kept: a tap brings the bar and the gesture back, and restoring it by hand would mean replacing the delegate of a navigation controller SwiftUI owns. Fading the bars' views by their alpha was tried and does not work: their glass ignores it until a scroll ends, and the bottom bar is a private floating view rather than the navigation controller's toolbar. Only on iPhone: an iPad in a compact width is where a keyboard is, and the bottom bar's shortcuts leave with its items.

The reader runs under both bars, so they are glass over the text rather than a solid strip above it, and their going changes only the safe area, never the reader's frame. `ReaderTextView` turns the safe area into content insets by hand, as `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior` is `.never`. The top inset moves `contentOffset`'s origin away from the top of the content, so the scroll arithmetic asks for the viewport through `PlatformTextView+Scrolling`, which measures it from below the top bar, as the Mac's does from below the toolbar.

While the bars are hidden the top inset holds at their height (`ReaderChrome.topInset`) instead of following the safe area in. Following it, a jump made while they were hidden was measured from below the status bar, and the bars it brought back then covered the heading it had landed on. Held, the bars going or coming back changes no inset at all, and what tracking reads as the top of the viewport stays below where the bar is or will be. Any other change of the top inset — the first layout, a rotation — moves the offset with it, so the line at the top stays: a place restored before the first inset arrived landed a bar's height too high. The bottom inset shrinks when the bottom bar's items go, and leaves the offset alone, so the text does not move. `ReaderChrome` measures scrolls by the offset, not by the distance from either end, which that moves, so the bars going is never taken for a scroll. They go no nearer either end than `ReaderChrome.hideFloor`, which is more than the bottom bar is tall, so the inset shrinking never lands the reader on the end, where the bars come straight back.

The header, a hosting controller inside the text view, has no safe area (`safeAreaRegions = []`). With one, scrolled under the top bar it was padded down by the overlap and measured with that padding, which became the text's top inset: a band of blank space above the title that stayed.
