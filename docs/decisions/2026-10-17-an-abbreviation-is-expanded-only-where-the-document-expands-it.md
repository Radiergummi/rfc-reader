# An abbreviation is expanded only where the document expands it

*Recorded 17 October 2026 from ARCHITECTURE.md (#756); decided with #67 and #690.*

Abbreviations are collected at parse time (`Abbreviations`, issue #67) from the document's own text: `Transport Layer Security (TLS)` at first use, and `TLS: Transport Layer Security` in a glossary definition list, where the whole opening phrase has to be the expansion. Both parsers set `RFCDocument.abbreviations` as their last step, keyed by the short form as written, and the first expansion wins. A section's heading is read before its body, as the reader meets it, so `3. Transport Layer Security (TLS)` gives the body's `TLS` its expansion, defined in that section; a heading's long form set all in capitals, as legacy headings often are (`RECIPIENT (RCPT)`), is kept only until a later expansion in mixed case, from the body or a later heading, replaces it. The long form is found with Schwartz and Hearst's algorithm, chosen for precision: every letter of the short form has to match, in order, and its first has to start a word, or there is no expansion. A wrong expansion shown confidently is worse than none. Four rules from a full-corpus measurement tighten it. An expansion never starts on a lowercase function word (`and Control of TE Networks (ACTN)` moves back to `Abstraction`); the word it moves back to cannot be a function word either, capitalized or not, and every other word it takes in has to begin with a letter of the short form, in order, so `all routers in a Border Network (ABN)` has no expansion rather than a wrong one. A hyphenated word is one word throughout, so neither a match nor that move lands inside `peer-to-peer`. The match takes the nearest letters, so a word can hold two of them and the expansion stop a word short (`Report` for `Receiver Report (RR)`, #690): where a word of the match holds two of the short form's capitals or digits, a part of a hyphenated word counting as a word and a part all in capitals, an acronym, not counting at all, and the match does not start on an acronym that supplies several letters (`SSH Fingerprint (SSHFP)`), the expansion starts one word earlier if that word starts with the first letter, is not a function word and leaves the initials following the short form less a plural `s`. That turns `Record` into `Resource Record` and `Unique Identifier` into `Universally Unique Identifier` across the corpus. Nothing holding `=`, `@` or `<` is an expansion. And a glossary phrase ends at `:`, at a dash set off by spaces (` -- `, ` - `) or an em dash, and before its trailing citations (`RFC 4655`, `RFC-4655`, `BCP 38`, `STD 5`, `I-D`, and a linked `RFC 4655 § 4` with its no-break spaces), which keeps out an IANA registration's `URI: urn:…` and an explanation after the expansion; what is left gets at most two more content words than the short form has letters, because a definition can be a sentence that happens to hold the letters. Bibliography entries and verbatim blocks are not read. The RPC's abbreviation list, the fallback for what a document never expands, waits on the licensing question in `DATA_PIPELINE.md`.
