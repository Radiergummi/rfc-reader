# How the legacy parser recovers structure

*Recorded 17 October 2026 from ARCHITECTURE.md (#756), which keeps a short account of each step. The issues named here hold the decisions and their measurements.*

## Depagination

Depagination: drop form feeds, `[Page N]` footers and the running header that follows a page break; remember where breaks were. Then drop the lines that recur at the page edges however they are worded (RFC 793's `Transmission Control Protocol`) — but only a line set off from the body by a blank and seen at the edges more often than inside the pages, because the body recurs too and a MIB's `STATUS current` lands at a page edge by chance. A line on half the pages names the document — half the pages of its parity, where headers alternate between facing pages (RFC 821). A section running header must head every page, or every other, of three or more, as part of the block that opens the page: the body starts below the blank lines after a page's header, and RFC 6208's `Additional information:` at the head of five pages is the body's. A line wrongly kept costs a stray heading; one wrongly dropped is lost text, so the rule errs towards keeping. Depagination does not decide a section running header's first sighting, which may be the only thing saying where its section starts (RFC 770's `References`) or a copy of a heading the document sets itself (RFC 793's `Philosophy`): it tags it (`Line.sectionHeader`), and `prepared` drops it later, through `Prelude`, where the document heads that section with the same words on the header's page or the two before it (#291). `corpus/report.json` records each document's dropped lines as `furniture`, because the block counts cannot show that loss.

## Headings

Headings: any non-indented line. `1.2.` → numbered; `Appendix B.` / `B.1.` → appendix; anything else unnumbered. Depth from the number; sections nest with a stack. `1:` and `2.4.12:` are numbered too, where the document numbers its headings that way (#71: RFC 2078, 2743 and 2130 all through, five others for a few). The same shape is a field label, a line-numbered listing or a second numbering elsewhere, so it counts only where two things hold across the document: no colon number is also the number of a `1.` heading, since a document numbers each section once (RFC 705 lists `1.  BEGIN Command` and describes it again under `1:  BEGIN`); and every colon number follows from a heading number — the one before it at its level, or one it is under — because a lone `11:` with no `10` is a time (RFC 526), not a numbering. `1)` is numbered by the same two rules, the first asked of every heading's number, one with no separator included, where RFC 1136, 1927 and 2122 head their sections so (#199), and by three more, since it is a list item in some forty other documents: each `1)` starts its block of two lines at most, no number comes twice, and the document names a section every RFC has, `Status of this Memo` or `Security Considerations`, which RFC 234's one-page agenda does not. A line ending in a contents leader and a page number (`2.  Overview ....... 5`) opens no section, wherever it sits (the front matter's end still counts it as a heading, so no front matter moves): RFC 791, 793 and the specifications set like them list their contents at column 0, and every entry opened a section (#403). Those documents center their chapter headings instead (`1.  INTRODUCTION`) and set only the subsections at column 0; a numbered line off column 0 is a heading where it stands alone, no heading has taken its number, and the next heading is its first subsection. Anywhere else it is a list item. Once a document has a centered chapter that a subsection confirms, a later line centered on the page, on its own and in capitals, can be a chapter heading too (#550). A numbered one needs a number between the confirmed chapters around it, so a figure's numbered row is not the next chapter; RFC 753's `4.  EXAMPLES & SCENARIOS` has no subsection to confirm it. An unnumbered one, or a centered `Appendix A`, comes after the last numbered heading, as RFC 793's `GLOSSARY` and `REFERENCES` do, whose references then become its bibliography; a centered caption inside a chapter would take the chapter's subsections under it. The capitals line right under a centered heading is that heading's title, and words spread across a line, a diagram's labels or a table's row, are not a title.

## Blocks

Blocks: runs of lines separated by blank lines. A block is prose if every line shares a small indent and contains no artwork tell-tales (`+--`, `|`, `->`, runs of spaces); prose is reflowed. A block starting with `o`, `-`, `*`, `1.`, `(a)` becomes a list. Everything else is preformatted, minus common indent. Blocks cut by a page break are re-joined when both halves look like prose and the first half does not end a sentence, or the second opens lower case; a trailing hyphen joins without a space. A second half that opens with a bullet is never joined, however the first ends (RFC 1581, 6614) — except a dash, which can continue a sentence (RFC 2123's `- e.g. its SourcePeerAddress - is ...`), and is refused only where the block above opens with a bullet too. A block indented past a list's marker and carrying no marker of its own is that item's next paragraph: the indent cap is excused, because the list above says what the indent means, but every other test of prose still applies — and one of them has to be `readsLikeSentences`, or the algorithm steps and tagged values that fill those indents are swallowed into the item as prose. Artwork shaped like a drawing (`DrawingShape`: mostly drawing characters, and not mostly lines of words) is typed `ascii-art`, RFCXML's type for one, so the model says which verbatim blocks are drawings (#361); `ArtworkType.declaresDrawing` reads it, while the classifier still takes `ascii-art` as saying nothing about what is drawn, so a packet diagram typed so is still recognized. A caption under a verbatim block, `Figure 3: Title` on a line or two or `Figure 3.` alone, makes the block a `Figure` with that number and title (`LegacyTextParser+Figures`), and `Table 3: Title` under a table titles it. Its ID is its part number, `pn="figure-3"`, as RFCXML's prep gives one, and no anchor: one spelled the same would declare the ID twice, so the first of two figures a document numbers alike keeps the number and the second its label. A caption with no title takes the line of words between it and a drawing, where there is one (RFC 793's `TCP Header Format`), and a note there stays in the figure; lines of words under anything but a drawing, or under nothing, leave the caption as it was, since they may be the prose of the code above. A figure numbered by section (`Figure 2.1`, RFC 1005) has no whole number for the model to hold, and keeps its label in its title.
