# A heading lists the sections that refer to it, through a chip

*Decided September 2026 (issue #183).* `Backlinks.within(_:)` in RFCKit reverses a document's own references: keyed by the section referred to, each value the citing sections in document order, the abstract first, with how often each cites. A reference to anything a section's own blocks anchor — a figure, a table, a paragraph — counts for that section; a subsection's references, made or received, are its own; a section referring to itself is left out. A bibliography entry is no target, since a citation names the work, not the row that lists it; and only what the reader draws in the body is a citing place, since a backlink has to lead somewhere: not a section that is only a bibliography (`Section.holdsOnlyReferences`, the reader's own test), nor a bibliography's annotations, which are the references panel's. A section with prose beside its bibliography is drawn, and its prose counts like any other. It is a pure function of the open document, walked as `proseInlinesBySection` walks it, less what the body leaves out, so it needs no pack; "cited by N RFCs" across the corpus is the corpus's to answer, and follows #174.

The reader shows it as a chip at the end of a heading, `↩ 3`, counting the citing sections — where the reader already is, rather than a count in the Contents panel or a line under every cited section. The chip is built by `DocumentTextBuilder` with the same `chipRun` as a reference's, so the body stays one storage, and its link is `rfc-backlinks:<anchor>`, which goes nowhere: the coordinator opens `BacklinksList`, the entries of `BuiltDocument.backlinks(of:)`, beside the chip, and a row jumps as a reference to that section would. It opens on a click or a tap, not on a hover as a reference card does, because its rows are buttons and a hover card closes as the pointer leaves the chip for it; on macOS it stays open while the pointer travels into it, as a document preview does. A build with no live links — print and export — has no chips. A copied heading leaves the chip and the space before it out, in the plain and the rich flavors (`.rfcBacklinks`, read by `SelectionText`); the chip, and the line break after it, carry neither the heading's `.rfcAnchor` nor its heading level, so the headings rotor reads the heading alone, as one stop. Its URL means nothing outside the reader, so on iOS it has no link menu, and on macOS a right-click keeps the text view's menu less its Copy Link item, matched by the `copyLink:` action (`BacklinkMenu`), so Copy and Copy as Quote stay for a selection. On macOS VoiceOver says the chip as "Referred to from 3 sections" ("1 section" for one) in place of its arrow and number, reading through and in the links rotor: the builder sets `AccessibleReading.backlinksLabel(count:)` on the chip as `.rfcSpoken`, which `AccessibleReading` says the way it says a diagram's label. On iOS `UITextView` has no per-range accessor to override, so there it still reads as the arrow and the number.
